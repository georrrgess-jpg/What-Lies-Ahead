# Style report: zombies and guns

*What Lies Ahead, v0.10.2*

## Overall art direction

The game is **stylised and toy-like**: chunky, rounded shapes, bold silhouettes, saturated
colours and a slightly glossy finish. It reads like vinyl collectible figures and toy
blasters, with no realism or gore. Every world gives this base look its own theme, and the
guns lean into carnival and arcade novelty.

## Zombies

### The shared base

- **Bodies:** big heads on compact bodies, grey-green skin with stitch marks, and large round
  cartoon eyes with tiny pupils that make them goofy rather than scary. Each world dresses this
  same body differently.
- **Readable from a distance:** each enemy type has one clear silhouette or prop, such as a hard
  hat, a riot shield, a ski suit or a wrecking ball.
- **Telegraphed attacks:** special attacks show a coloured floor ring, a fan or a glow before
  they land, so threats read at a glance. Damage looks cartoonish, with no blood.
- **Bosses** are larger, armour-plated versions with their own props and a dark tint, so they
  stand out from the crowd.

### Assessment

**What works:** costume humour (tourists with cameras, a mall walker pumping its arms, a
commuter with a lanyard and coffee), strong world identity, and threats that are fair to read.

**Where it's weaker:** the shared base body means regular zombies across worlds can feel samey
in shape; their outfits differ more than their builds. Ashfall City's enemies are the most
"grounded" designs, which suits the darker setting but sits a little apart from the carnival
worlds.

## Guns

### The shared look

- **Shapes:** chunky toy-blaster proportions with oversized parts (drums, hoppers, tanks,
  coils), rounded edges and candy-bright colours over dark grips.
- **Glowing details:** energy cells, displays and tubes glow, giving a sci-fi arcade feel.
- **Animations:** every gun has its own model, a holographic reload and its own sounds and
  reticle. Viewmodels sway, kick and recover smoothly, and the real sight lines up when you aim
  down sights.

### Primaries (12)

- **Grounded sci-fi:** Ion Carbine, Scatter Blaster, Arc Coil (chain lightning), Frostbite Cryo
  Beam, Rail Lancer.
- **Carnival and novelty:** Bumper Lobber (gumball bombs), Gumball Drum Popper, Teacup Twister
  (spinning teacup whirl), Whirligig (prize-wheel minigun), Riptide Harpoon.
- **Hybrid:** Toxic Ripsaw (slime shotgun with a chainsaw).
- **Endless reward:** Road Rager (a hot-rod pitching machine that throws a boomerang hubcap).

### Secondaries (7)

Pulse Pistol, Comet Hand Cannon, Dunk Tank (water jet), Shutterbug (flash camera), Sparkler
(firework machine pistol), Ring Toss (ricochet hoop), Balloon Darter.

### Cosmetics

Cosmetics extend the style:

- around 400 finishes, sights, energy cells, charms and decals
- Prize Prints with rolled patterns
- 8 Ashfall City camos
- the animated Mythic finish **Last Light** (a burning skyline on black chrome)

### Assessment

**What works:** every gun has a clear gimmick you can see in the model itself (a teacup, a
hubcap, a prize wheel), so silhouette and mechanic match. The colours match the worlds, and the
novelty weapons give the game its personality.

**Where it's weaker:**

- **Mixed families:** the early sci-fi guns (Ion Carbine, Rail Lancer) are more conventional
  than the carnival-themed later ones, so the arsenal mixes two styles.
- **Tiny thumbnails:** some models read small in the Arsenal and Locker thumbnails, where fine
  detail turns to speckles.

## Summary

- **Zombies:** goofy, costumed toy-figure undead with readable silhouettes and telegraphed
  attacks, consistent across 11 worlds.
- **Guns:** chunky, glowing toy blasters with visible gimmicks, moving from clean sci-fi to
  carnival novelty.
